Risk · R-003 · Assessed 2026-10-30

Breached payroll provider still connected to HRHigh today, medium after fixes.

The ransomware gang that hit our payroll provider last month could reuse the provider's live link into our HR system to steal all staff records and divert a payroll run. The link can write bank details, its key hasn't been reset since the attack, and nothing alerts on changes. Pausing the link and resetting its key closes the way in today.

Owner HR DirectorStatus openDue 2026-11-02 · 2026-11-20 · 2026-12-31Frameworks ISO 27001 · NIS2 · DORA · GDPR

— Scenario

  • Risk source: the ransomware gang that hit the payroll provider last month. We have no proof it has been removed from the provider's systems.
  • Feared event: staff personal and bank data stolen, and bank details changed so salaries go to the gang. Secondary route: a fake employee gets a real Microsoft 365 login, then AWS through single sign-on. Hire approval blocks most of this.
  • Asset: the HR system, with ~200 staff records (identity, address, bank details). It is also the source for automatic account creation.
  • Why it works: the link can write, its key is unchanged since the breach and its network limits are unknown. No bank-change check, no alerts, and no supplier-access rule in access-control policy v2.1.
  • Business value: staff trust and payroll continuity. A leak triggers GDPR breach notification within 72 hours (Art. 33–34), and falls under DORA Art. 28 and NIS2 Art. 21(2)(d).
