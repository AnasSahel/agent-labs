— Executive summary

What could happen
Someone using a former developer's still-active GitHub account changes the payment code. It reaches production with no second approval and quietly diverts or stops payments. Critical: lost money, a reportable incident, licence and customer trust at stake.
Why now
The account was found still active with write access. No rule removes access when people leave, and nothing requires a second approval.
Where we stand
G4·V3 high today, above appetite → G4·V1 medium after fixes, within appetite.
What we need
The CTO removes the account today, then ships 6 fixes by 2026-11-30. Mostly settings changes; security key cost not yet estimated.

— Scenario

  • Risk source: the former developer, or anyone who takes over their account (stolen password, SMS code, old token or SSH key).
  • Feared event: the payment service is altered: payments diverted to an attacker's account, or stopped.
  • Asset: the payment service and its code repository.
  • Why it works: the account was never removed (access-control policy v2.1 has no leaver rule; reviews run every 6 months). SMS is allowed as a second factor. A push reaches production with no second approval.
  • Business value: the service moves customer money and holds customer payment data. A diverting change is a major ICT incident (DORA Art. 19), a significant incident (NIS2 Art. 23), and a personal data breach when customer data is touched (GDPR Art. 33).
